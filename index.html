Baytak & Matrahak بيتك ومطرحك

An Egyptian e-commerce brand concept built around everyday problem-solving. It curates smart, practical products that make everyday life easier, especially for women across different lifestyles: single or married, working or homemakers. Every product is chosen to save time, reduce effort, and simplify daily life.

Problem, audience & context

AudienceMothers of children whose smartphone use interferes with homework.

What everyday task can be made easier?

An agile launch strategy centered on time-saving household solutions for women, using a lean validation framework to test product demand with minimal capital risk.

Before a word is written, each project is framed by five questions: what problem the audience has, who exactly the audience is, how aware they are, where this piece sits in the funnel, and what the copy has to make them do.

Read the copy

Buyer and user can be different people. In the case study the buyer is the mother and the user is the child, and the copy speaks to the buyer.

Research that finds the real struggle

VOCReview customer discussions and reviews to identify recurring situations, frustrations, failed attempts, and objections.
Competitor researchAnalyze how existing solutions communicate: recurring angles, promises, alternatives, and unanswered objections.
Research decision. Every research pass ends with a decision that changes the creative direction. In the case study it shifted the message from "Take the phone away" to "Give the child a better alternative."

From insight to decision

Each insight is tested against VOC and competitor research, then converted into a decision the creative can act on.

The phone needs a replacementThe problem is not only screen time; the mother needs something to keep the child engaged once the phone is gone.
Homework is the entry pointThe phone becomes a bigger problem when it competes with something the mother already expects.
Removing creates a gapTaking the phone away leaves a hole if nothing engaging replaces it.
Fit the routineA solution feels relevant when the mother sees how it fits a familiar moment at home.

Strategic decisions

Shift the problem frame
Insight

The problem is not simply excessive screen time. The mother also needs a practical alternative to keep the child engaged when the phone is taken away.

Decision

Move away from "Take the phone away" and toward "Give the child a better alternative."

Why

A message focused only on removing the phone addresses the behavior, but not the gap that follows.

Creative direction

Position the solution as something that can replace part of the role the phone plays in the child's routine.

Lead with the homework conflict
Insight

Smartphone use becomes a bigger problem when it competes with something the mother already expects, especially homework.

Decision

Use the homework-vs-phone conflict as the primary entry point instead of the broader issue of screen time.

Why

"Too much screen time" is a broad concern. Homework is a specific, recognizable situation where the mother can immediately see herself.

Creative direction

Start from a familiar moment: the child is supposed to be doing homework, but the phone keeps winning his attention.

Show the alternative, not just the problem
Insight

Taking the phone away creates a gap if nothing engaging replaces it.

Decision

Demonstrate what the child can do instead, rather than spending the communication on the negative effects of smartphone use.

Why

Repeating the problem doesn't answer the mother's practical question: "Okay, if I take the phone away, what will he do instead?"

Creative direction

Make the alternative visible and concrete, so the role of the solution is understood through its use.

Demonstrate through a real-life use case
Insight

A solution becomes more relevant when the mother can immediately understand how it fits a familiar situation at home.

Research decision

Show the problem, the alternative, and the outcome rather than leading with product features.

How I work

Strong copy starts with the right strategic decision, not just better wording. This is the process behind every piece.

  1. Start with the business

    Understand the business goal, the offer, the audience, and what the copy needs to achieve.

  2. Research before writing

    Research the customer, VOC, competitors, and market to find the strongest opportunities.

  3. Build the direction

    Turn what we learn into an insight, an angle, and a message, then pick the direction that serves both customer and business.

  4. Write & present

    Develop the copy and present it in the context where it will be used.
